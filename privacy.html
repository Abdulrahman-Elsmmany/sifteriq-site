The short version

  • Sifteriq helps you organize your own YouTube Watch Later list. It uses YouTube API Services, and Google's Privacy Policy applies to data that YouTube processes.
  • We store your email address, the videos you import and their public YouTube details, what you write and choose in Sifteriq (such as notes, decisions, your goal, saved views and snoozes), where you stopped in videos you play in Sifteriq, and AI results.
  • If you turn on AI recommendations and agree to the AI notice Sifteriq shows first, we send video details and your goal through OpenRouter to AI providers that summarize your videos, rank them for your goal, turn text into embeddings and find saved videos that cover the same thing (Groups). Each provider is named in Who processes it. We never send your name, email address, notes, watch progress or account identifiers. The AI providers keep none of it after responding. OpenRouter, which forwards each request, sorts a small sample of requests into anonymous categories for its public usage rankings.
  • We do not sell your data, show our own ads, or use analytics or tracking cookies. When you play a video in Sifteriq, YouTube's player loads and follows Google's Privacy Policy (see Playing videos in Sifteriq).
  • AI features run only if you turn them on, within a monthly AI budget for each account. Data from YouTube is refreshed or deleted within 30 days, with three exceptions today, described in 30-day refresh or deletion.
  • You can export your data, disconnect YouTube, revoke access in your Google account, clear your library, or delete your account.
  • Clearing your library deletes your imported videos, notes, decisions, watch plans, watch progress, snoozes, saved exports, import history and AI results within 7 days. It keeps your account, your YouTube connection, your saved views (without any channel they filter by), your service, usage and security records, and your recent goals with their AI profiles. Deleting your account deletes your data within 7 days, except our sign-in provider's own service logs. After that deletion, apart from those logs, the only records that stay longer than 7 days show that a deletion happened, or are public video details that are no longer linked to you and follow the 30-day rule; none of them identifies you (see Deleting your data).
  • Disconnecting YouTube in Sifteriq also deletes the videos imported through that connection, normally at once. Today, revoking access in your Google account, or switching to a different Google account in Sifteriq, ends Sifteriq's access to that account but keeps the videos imported through it (see How to revoke access). At launch A disconnect's deletion always completes within 7 days, and revoking access in your Google account or switching to a different Google account also deletes the videos imported through that connection.

Required disclosures

YouTube API Services and your Google data

This section brings together what YouTube and Google require us to tell you.

Sifteriq uses YouTube API Services

Sifteriq uses YouTube API Services. YouTube API Services are provided by Google. Google's Privacy Policy is at http://www.google.com/policies/privacy. It applies to the data that Google and YouTube process when Sifteriq calls YouTube for you, and when you play a video in Sifteriq with YouTube's embedded player.

By using Sifteriq, you agree to be bound by the YouTube Terms of Service at https://www.youtube.com/t/terms. See also our Terms of Service.

What Sifteriq accesses from YouTube and Google

Sifteriq does not use Google sign-in. You sign in with an email address and password. Connecting a Google account is optional and is used only to connect a YouTube playlist.

Video details, for every video you import. However you import a video, Sifteriq asks the YouTube Data API for its public details using Sifteriq's own API key: title, description, tags, channel name and ID, category, language, duration, publish date, privacy and availability status, whether it may be embedded, whether YouTube designates it as made for kids, and thumbnail links. Sifteriq asks again before the details are 30 days old, so they stay current, and deletes them if it cannot. These requests tell Google which videos were looked up, not which Sifteriq user asked.

If you connect a YouTube account. Sifteriq asks Google for one of two permissions:

We store it encrypted (AES-256-GCM) in our database at Supabase, and the Sifteriq web page can never read it. Short-lived access tokens are never saved: the Sifteriq app keeps them only in memory while it runs (today, on the developer's computer).

Sifteriq does not read your watch history, subscriptions, comments, likes or YouTube Analytics through the API, and it never downloads videos, captions or transcripts. YouTube does not let apps read the Watch Later list through the API, so you bring it in yourself: upload a Google Takeout export, paste links, or copy the videos to a normal playlist and connect that playlist. A Google Takeout file you upload is handled differently from the API: see Google Takeout files in Information we collect.

Actions Sifteriq takes on your YouTube account

Only with the Manage permission, and only after you start an action in Sifteriq:

  • Create a private playlist named "Sifteriq Inbox", when you choose to create it. If your account already has a private playlist with that name, Sifteriq reuses it. Either way, it becomes the playlist Sifteriq syncs.
  • Remove videos from the playlist you connected: only the videos you select, and only after you confirm the removal. Removal changes the playlist on YouTube.
  • Undo a removal: put a video you removed back at the end of the playlist, when you choose Undo.

Switching to a different Google account creates no playlist by itself; you pick one from the new account. If you choose Create a private Sifteriq Inbox and then pick a different account at Google, Sifteriq creates or reuses that playlist in the account you picked. Sifteriq never uploads, rates or comments on videos, and never changes any other playlist or setting. Sifteriq may suggest removing a video and show why, but nothing is ever removed on its own: only a Remove button you press, and confirm, removes a video.

Playing videos in Sifteriq

Sifteriq can play videos from your library inside Sifteriq with YouTube's embedded player in privacy-enhanced mode (youtube-nocookie.com) and YouTube's IFrame Player API. YouTube provides the player, and Sifteriq shows it unchanged, with YouTube's own controls and any ads YouTube shows. When it loads and while you watch, YouTube may collect information about your device and your viewing, and may place, read or recognize cookies or similar technologies on your device, as described in the Google Privacy Policy (http://www.google.com/policies/privacy). The player loads only when you choose to watch a video, and a video starts only when you choose to play it, for example with the play button or by choosing one of its chapters.

For videos that YouTube has not designated as made for kids, Sifteriq saves where you stopped and how long you played (about every 30 seconds while a video plays, and when you pause, close the player or leave the page), and marks a video watched when you reach its end after playing most of it; you can undo that. Only you can see this record. Sifteriq uses it to resume a video where you stopped, to show what you started on Today, and to choose which video of a group in Groups to start with. It is Sifteriq's own record, not data from YouTube, and it is never sent to AI providers. Sifteriq deletes it 28 days after it was last saved, when the video leaves your library (for example when you clear your library or disconnect YouTube), when you undo a watched mark, and when you delete your account, and within 7 days of a deletion request.

Sifteriq does not play videos designated as made for kids, or videos YouTube does not allow to be embedded, inside Sifteriq. They open on YouTube instead, and Sifteriq records nothing about them while they play. When Sifteriq does not yet know whether a video is made for kids, it first asks the YouTube Data API for that video's details (one request), and if it still cannot tell, opens the video on YouTube.

How to revoke access

You can withdraw Sifteriq's access to your Google account at any time, in either of two ways:

  • In Sifteriq: go to Settings → Connections and choose Disconnect YouTube. Sifteriq asks Google to revoke its access straight away and deletes its stored credentials. Settings → Connections also links to Google's Privacy Policy and to the Google security settings page.
  • In your Google account: in addition to deleting your data in Sifteriq, you can revoke Sifteriq's access to your Google account data at any time on the Google security settings page.

Google security settingshttps://security.google.com/settings/security/permissions

When you disconnect YouTube in Sifteriq, it also deletes the YouTube data obtained through that connection, normally at once: your channel and playlist details and the videos imported from your connected playlists, together with anything attached to those videos in Sifteriq, such as notes, decisions, watch progress, snoozes, AI results and saved watch plans that include them, and your saved export files. Videos you also imported another way, for example from a Takeout file, stay in your library. Today, if Sifteriq's access ends at Google instead, for example because you revoked it on Google's security settings page, or if you switch to a different Google account in Sifteriq, the videos imported through that connection stay until you clear your library, disconnect YouTube in Sifteriq or delete your account, and the IDs of its channel and playlists stay until you disconnect YouTube in Sifteriq or delete your account. Today the same applies to a playlist you replace by choosing a different one for Sifteriq to sync, for example with Change playlist in Settings → Connections: the videos imported from it, and its ID, stay in the same way. At launch If a disconnect's deletion cannot finish at once, for example while a sync is running, it completes within 7 calendar days. If you revoke access on Google's security settings page instead, Sifteriq detects it and deletes that data within 30 calendar days; reconnecting the same account before then keeps it. Switching to a different Google account in Sifteriq ends the previous account's connection in the same way: unless you switch back to that account within 3 days, its data is deleted within 7 calendar days of the switch.

Deleting your data

You can delete the data Sifteriq stores at any time:

  • Clear library (Settings → Data and privacy) deletes your imported videos, notes, decisions, watch plans, watch progress, snoozes, saved exports, import history, your choices in Groups and the AI results for your videos (summaries, rankings, embeddings and Groups answers). It keeps:
    • your account, YouTube connection, sync history, YouTube quota use and security events;
    • your saved views, without any channel they filter by;
    • the goals you used recently, with their AI profiles, until they are deleted as described in How long we keep information;
    • usage information about past AI requests, such as token counts and cost, and your monthly AI budgets, which are needed to enforce budgets and usage limits. These records hold no video details and no AI text.
  • Delete account data (Settings → Data and privacy) asks Google to revoke Sifteriq's access, deletes your library, exports, YouTube credentials and account, and signs you out on every device.
  • By email: write to abdoelsmany66@gmail.com from the address on your account.

Both in-app actions ask you to confirm, and to have signed in with your password within the last 10 minutes. They take effect immediately.

When you ask us to delete your data, or you delete your account, we delete the related data we store as soon as possible and within 7 calendar days. Two kinds of record are exceptions: the public video details that your imports stored, which are stored once for every user who saved the same video and follow the 30-day rule (see below); and our sign-in provider's own service logs, which keep entries for a short period set by our Supabase plan (see Sign-in records below). Records that show a deletion happened are also kept; they identify no one (see below).

Backups made by our database provider are kept for no more than 7 days, so deleted data also leaves them within that time.

Deleting the data Sifteriq stores does not affect any data stored by YouTube. To delete data on YouTube, use YouTube or another authorized app that can delete it.

Public video details, such as titles, descriptions, tags and channel names, are stored once and reused for every user who saved the same video; other users cannot see that you saved it. After you delete your account they are no longer linked to you. They follow the 30-day rule below, and are deleted once no one's library holds the video.

Sifteriq keeps two of its own records after account deletion: a record that your library was cleared, which holds counts of what was deleted; and a record that the account was deleted, which holds the date, counts of what was deleted and whether Google confirmed the revocation. Neither holds your email address or any other identifier, any content, or any data from YouTube. They are kept with no time limit, to show the deletion happened.

If Google does not confirm the revocation, Sifteriq tells you and links to the Google security settings page so you can remove its access yourself.

Sign-in records. Our sign-in provider, Supabase, keeps a log of account events: sign-ups, sign-ins, session refreshes, sign-outs, password resets and changes, and the deletion of your account. Each entry holds your email address. Supabase keeps this log only in its own service logs, not in our database. Those logs also record IP addresses and browser types, and keep entries for a short period set by our Supabase plan, including the entry for the deletion of your account.

Sifteriq keeps data it obtains from YouTube API Services for no more than 30 calendar days before it refreshes that data from YouTube or deletes it. This covers video details, channel and playlist details, copies of that data inside Sifteriq, such as watch-plan snapshots, and what Sifteriq derives from it, such as AI summaries, embeddings, Groups answers and groups of duplicate copies: these are deleted when the details they were made from are not refreshed in time. Details of videos that become private, are deleted from YouTube, or are no longer in anyone's library are deleted from Sifteriq.

Today there are three exceptions:

  • Ended connections and replaced playlists. When you choose a different playlist for Sifteriq to sync, for example with Change playlist in Settings → Connections, or a YouTube connection ends other than through Disconnect YouTube, because Sifteriq's access ended at Google or you switched to a different Google account in Sifteriq, Sifteriq keeps the IDs of the playlists it no longer syncs and of an ended connection's channel, and the list of videos imported from those playlists, as described in How to revoke access. Their names and other details from YouTube still follow the rule above.
  • Saved export files. A file can no longer be downloaded 23 hours after it is made. The file itself is then deleted by a cleanup that runs in Sifteriq's background worker, which today runs only on the developer's computer, or when you make another export, clear your library, disconnect YouTube or delete your account. While none of these happens, a file can stay longer than 30 days (see Saved export files in How long we keep information).
  • The cached copy of your library. Sifteriq's pages read your library from a cached copy, which is rebuilt from the refreshed or deleted details when the background worker runs or you open Sifteriq. While neither happens, it can keep video details, such as titles and descriptions, for longer than 30 days.

At launch The data of an ended connection is deleted as described in How to revoke access, each saved export file is deleted within 24 hours after it is made, and the data of a playlist you replaced and the cached copy of your library follow the rule above.

The list of videos you imported from a file or pasted links is your own data, as are your notes, decisions, goal, saved views and snoozes. The videos, notes and decisions stay until you clear your library or delete your account, and your goal until you delete your account. You can edit or clear a note, and edit your goal, at any time. Watch progress is your own record too, but Sifteriq deletes it 28 days after it was last saved (see Playing videos in Sifteriq). When a video's details change, Sifteriq summarizes it again if AI recommendations are on; the replaced summary, and the rankings made from it, are deleted within 2 days.

AI processing

Sifteriq's AI features run only if you turn on AI recommendations and agree to the AI notice Sifteriq shows first, which lists each request below, what it sends and who receives it. With your agreement, Sifteriq uses AI models to describe each saved video, understand your goal, rank your videos for it, and find saved videos that cover the same thing (Groups). There is one ranking system: every recommendation comes from these steps. A video the AI has not reached yet shows as "not ranked yet", with the reason.

What is sent, step by step

  • Video summaries, to one of the AI model providers: for each video, its title, channel name, duration, publish year and how old it is (under 12 months, 12 to 18 months, or over 18 months), up to 16 of its YouTube tags and the first 1,200 characters of its description, in requests of up to 20 videos. Each video is labeled with a temporary number (v01, v02 and so on), not its YouTube ID. Your goal is not sent. The AI writes a short summary of each video: its topic, names it mentions, what it covers, what it is worth watching for, its type, its level and how time-sensitive it is.
  • Your goal, to one of the AI model providers: the goal you wrote, on its own, so the AI turns it into a short profile of what it asks for (topics, level, preferred length, how recent, what to leave out). Sifteriq shows this profile in Settings.
  • Ranking, to one of the AI model providers: your goal with Sifteriq's summaries of up to 100 videos a request and each video's length and age, each under a temporary number, so the AI ranks the videos for your goal and gives a short reason. Titles, descriptions and video IDs are not sent with these requests.
  • Embeddings, to DeepInfra or Nebius: for each video, its title, chapter titles, the first 500 characters of its description (without links, hashtags or timestamps) and up to 16 of its tags; its title alone and its topic from the summary (up to 300 characters each); and your goal on its own. They turn each text into a list of numbers (an embedding). Sifteriq uses these to compare videos with your goal and to find videos that may cover the same thing, and never shows or exports them.
  • Groups, to TypeSafe and Microsoft Azure: see Groups below.
  • Never sent: your name, email address, user ID, YouTube channel, video, channel or playlist IDs, playlist names, notes, watch progress, snoozes, saved views or uploaded files.

Groups: saved videos that cover the same thing

Groups shows saved videos that cover the same thing, such as the same release, news story, talk or question, so you can start with one and see what each of the others adds. To find them, a small model that runs on Sifteriq's own worker, with no network access, compares the embeddings and the videos' details and picks likely pairs. For each likely pair, Sifteriq sends:

  • To TypeSafe (its Jev model), for each of the two videos: its title (up to 300 characters), publish date, length, language, up to 8 of its tags (leaving out tags its channel puts on most of its videos), the start of its description (up to 500 characters, without links, timestamp lines or lines its channel repeats), its chapter titles (up to 300 characters) and its topic from Sifteriq's summary; and four facts Sifteriq works out: whether both come from the same channel, whether their lengths match, how far apart their lengths are and the days between their publish dates. TypeSafe's model answers a fixed set of questions about the pair, one pair per request.
  • To Microsoft Azure (OpenAI's GPT-6.1 Sol model running on Azure), only for the pairs TypeSafe's answers leave uncertain: the same details without the topics, up to 10 pairs per request, each pair under a temporary number. The model decides how each pair relates.

Groups requests never include channel names, video, channel or playlist IDs, or your goal. Sifteriq stores each answer with a fingerprint (hash) of its exact input and the model that answered, so it never pays to ask the same question twice, and stores the pairs decided to cover the same thing. Groups themselves are worked out each time you open them and are not stored; the server keeps the last result in memory for up to a minute. Sifteriq deletes each answer and decision 28 days after the earlier time it fetched the two videos' details from YouTube (unless a refresh finds both unchanged), when either video's details are not refreshed within 28 days, and when either video leaves your library.

Temporary files. While the Groups step runs, Sifteriq's background worker keeps one folder for your library on its own disk, with a working copy of your videos' details, summaries, embeddings and stored answers. It deletes the working copy as soon as it has read the result, or when the step fails; keeps only the scores of your current library and a copy of your embeddings that matches the database; and deletes the whole folder when Groups cannot run for you (for example after you turn AI recommendations off), after the deletion that follows a disconnect, and at its next maintenance pass (about every 5 minutes while the worker runs) once the folder has not been used for 24 hours or you have cleared your library, disconnected YouTube or deleted your account. Today the worker runs only on the developer's computer, so it deletes these files the next time it runs.

Monthly AI budgets

Sifteriq is free, so each account has monthly budgets for what its AI requests may cost: one for the summaries, goal profile, ranking and embeddings, and one for Groups, both larger in the month you sign up. Daily limits and a limit for all accounts together also apply. A request that would go over a budget is not sent: Sifteriq pauses that step until the budget renews, keeps what it already made, and shows why in the app. Sifteriq records each request's tokens and cost to enforce these budgets (see Information we collect).

Who processes it

OpenRouter, Inc. (United States), an AI gateway, forwards each request to a model provider that runs the model and returns the result. OpenRouter also sorts a small sample of requests into anonymous categories, using a model that keeps no data, for its public usage rankings. Sifteriq does not train any AI model on your data.

OpenRouter sends each request for video summaries, goal profiles and ranking to one of four model providers: Together AI, Makora, Ionstream and Parasail. Together AI, Ionstream and Parasail are based in the United States; Makora is run by A2 Labs Inc., whose privacy policy names service providers in the United States. It sends each embedding request to one of two model providers: DeepInfra, run by DeepInfra, Inc., based in the United States; or Nebius, run by Nebius Group N.V., based in the Netherlands, whose privacy policy names group companies in the Netherlands, Israel and the United States. It sends each Groups question to TypeSafe, run by Typesafe AI, Inc., whose services are hosted in the United States; and each uncertain Groups pair to Microsoft Azure, run by Microsoft Corporation, based in the United States, at an Azure endpoint in the United States or the European Union, or at one whose region OpenRouter does not state. Sifteriq asks for zero data retention on every request, so OpenRouter sends it only to endpoints that OpenRouter lists as keeping no prompts or outputs after responding. Your data is therefore not stored by these model providers or used by them to train AI models.

Your choice

AI recommendations are off until you turn them on. Sifteriq shows the AI notice and asks before it first sends your videos to an AI provider, and you can turn AI recommendations off at any time with the AI recommendations switch in Settings → Account. Sifteriq then sends none of these requests, Groups included. Results it already made stay in your library and are kept as described in How long we keep information. When a new version of the notice names a new recipient or a new kind of data, Sifteriq stops sending until you agree to it. Without AI you can still import, organize, review and play your videos yourself, and Sifteriq still finds duplicate copies by its fixed rules.

When AI recommendations are on, your goal goes to the AI model providers for its profile and for ranking, and to the embedding providers. Please do not put sensitive personal information in it, such as health details, religious or political views, or anything that identifies you.

AI labels are Sifteriq's own estimates

Sifteriq's video summaries (what a video covers and what it is worth watching for), fit labels, rankings, reasons, goal profiles, Groups (which videos cover the same thing, the video to start with and what each other video adds) and duplicate-copy groups are generated independently by Sifteriq and are not sourced directly from YouTube's API. YouTube does not provide or endorse them. They can be wrong, so check them before you act on them, especially before removing videos from YouTube. Sifteriq labels these fields in the app and in exports as its own estimates, not data from YouTube. Watch progress and watched marks are Sifteriq's own record of what you played, not a YouTube metric.

Sifteriq's AI describes and compares videos, not people. The profile of a goal describes what the goal asks for, not you. Sifteriq does not use AI to profile you or to infer sensitive characteristics about you. Watch plans, which follow your rankings, and the video a group starts with are put together by fixed rules, without a further AI request; they may use your watch progress.

Security

We protect your information with measures that include:

  • Google refresh tokens encrypted at rest (AES-256-GCM) and never readable from the browser;
  • database row-level security, so each account can read only its own data;
  • administrative keys kept on the server only;
  • protection against cross-site request forgery and clickjacking, and a strict content security policy;
  • a recent password sign-in before you can clear your library or delete your account;
  • limits on upload sizes and request rates, and a confirmation before every removal from YouTube;
  • At launch HTTPS encryption for every connection to the app.

No system is perfectly secure. If a security breach affects your personal information, we will tell you, and the relevant authorities, where the law requires it.

When people can see your data

Sifteriq is run by one developer, who has administrative access to its database in order to operate the service. We do not read your information, including data from your Google account, except:

  • when you ask us to, for example to help with a support request;
  • when it is needed for security, such as investigating abuse or a bug;
  • to comply with the law;
  • in aggregated, anonymized form for running the service, such as total usage counts.
