Privacy Policy

Effective date: 8 October 2026 Last updated: 13 October 2026

Sifteriq is in private development and is not yet available to the public.

No one other than its developer uses Sifteriq today. This policy describes how Sifteriq handles personal data, including how it will handle your data when it opens to users. Commitments marked "At launch" describe practices that are not yet in place. Sifteriq will not open to anyone other than its developer until they are in place, and we will remove each label as the practice it marks goes live. A label applies to everything after it, up to the end of its paragraph, list item or table cell, and to any list that the labeled sentence introduces. A label on a table row's heading applies to the whole row.

The short version

  • Sifteriq helps you organize your own YouTube Watch Later list. It uses YouTube API Services, and Google's Privacy Policy applies to data that YouTube processes.
  • We store your email address, the videos you import and their public YouTube details, what you write in Sifteriq, and AI results.
  • If you turn on AI triage, we send video details and your goal to an AI provider to rank your videos. If you agree to our AI notice of 13 October 2026, we also send them to AI providers to summarize and compare your videos, rank them for your goal and check possible duplicates, including to DeepInfra and Nebius, which turn text into embeddings (see Embeddings, video summaries and goal ranking). We never send your name, email address, notes or account identifiers. The AI model providers keep none of it after responding. OpenRouter, which forwards each request, sorts a small sample of requests into anonymous categories for its public usage rankings (see Who processes it).
  • We do not sell your data, show ads, or use analytics or tracking cookies.
  • AI triage runs only if you turn it on. Data from YouTube is refreshed or deleted within 30 days, with three exceptions today, described in 30-day refresh or deletion.
  • You can export your data, disconnect YouTube, revoke access in your Google account, clear your library, or delete your account.
  • Clearing your library deletes your imported videos, notes, decisions, watch plans, saved exports, import history and AI results within 7 days. It keeps your account, your YouTube connection, your service, usage and security records, and, if you agreed to the AI notice of 13 October 2026, your recent goals and their AI profiles. Deleting your account deletes your data within 7 days, except our sign-in provider's own service logs. After that deletion, apart from those logs, the only records that stay longer than 7 days show that a deletion happened, or are public video details that are no longer linked to you and follow the 30-day rule; none of them identifies you (see Deleting your data).
  • Disconnecting YouTube in Sifteriq also deletes the videos imported through that connection, normally at once. Today, revoking access in your Google account, or switching to a different Google account in Sifteriq, ends Sifteriq's access to that account but keeps the videos imported through it (see How to revoke access). At launch A disconnect's deletion always completes within 7 days, and revoking access in your Google account or switching to a different Google account also deletes the videos imported through that connection.

Required disclosures

YouTube API Services and your Google data

This section brings together what YouTube and Google require us to tell you.

Sifteriq uses YouTube API Services

Sifteriq uses YouTube API Services. YouTube API Services are provided by Google. Google's Privacy Policy is at http://www.google.com/policies/privacy. It applies to the data that Google and YouTube process when Sifteriq calls YouTube for you.

By using Sifteriq, you agree to be bound by the YouTube Terms of Service at https://www.youtube.com/t/terms. See also our Terms of Service.

What Sifteriq accesses from YouTube and Google

Sifteriq does not use Google sign-in. You sign in with an email address and password. Connecting a Google account is optional and is used only to connect a YouTube playlist.

Video details, for every video you import. However you import a video, Sifteriq asks the YouTube Data API for its public details using Sifteriq's own API key: title, description, tags, channel name and ID, category, language, duration, publish date, privacy and availability status, and thumbnail links. These requests tell Google which videos were looked up, not which Sifteriq user asked.

If you connect a YouTube account. Sifteriq asks Google for one of two permissions:

When you connect, Google gives Sifteriq a refresh token so it can keep your playlist in sync. We store it encrypted (AES-256-GCM) in our database at Supabase, and the Sifteriq web page can never read it. Short-lived access tokens are never saved: the Sifteriq app keeps them only in memory while it runs (today, on the developer's computer).

Sifteriq does not read your watch history, subscriptions, comments, likes or YouTube Analytics through the API, and it never downloads videos. YouTube does not let apps read the Watch Later list through the API, so you bring it in yourself: upload a Google Takeout export, paste links, or copy the videos to a normal playlist and connect that playlist. A Google Takeout file you upload is handled differently from the API: see Google Takeout files in Information we collect.

Actions Sifteriq takes on your YouTube account

Only with the Manage permission, and only after you start an action in Sifteriq:

  • Create a private playlist named "Sifteriq Inbox", when you choose to create it. If your account already has a private playlist with that name, Sifteriq reuses it. Either way, it becomes the playlist Sifteriq syncs.
  • Remove videos from the playlist you connected: only the videos you select, and only after you confirm the removal. Removal changes the playlist on YouTube.
  • Undo a removal: put a video you removed back at the end of the playlist, when you choose Undo.

Switching to a different Google account creates no playlist by itself; you pick one from the new account. If you choose Create a private Sifteriq Inbox and then pick a different account at Google, Sifteriq creates or reuses that playlist in the account you picked. Sifteriq never uploads, rates or comments on videos, and never changes any other playlist or setting. AI suggestions never remove anything on their own.

How to revoke access

You can withdraw Sifteriq's access to your Google account at any time, in either of two ways:

  • In Sifteriq: go to Settings → Connections and choose Disconnect YouTube. Sifteriq asks Google to revoke its access straight away and deletes its stored credentials. Settings → Connections also links to Google's Privacy Policy and to the Google security settings page.
  • In your Google account: in addition to deleting your data in Sifteriq, you can revoke Sifteriq's access to your Google account data at any time on the Google security settings page.

Google security settingshttps://security.google.com/settings/security/permissions

When you disconnect YouTube in Sifteriq, it also deletes the YouTube data obtained through that connection, normally at once: your channel and playlist details and the videos imported from your connected playlists, together with anything attached to those videos in Sifteriq, such as notes, decisions, AI results and saved watch plans that include them, and your saved export files. Videos you also imported another way, for example from a Takeout file, stay in your library. Today, if Sifteriq's access ends at Google instead, for example because you revoked it on Google's security settings page, or if you switch to a different Google account in Sifteriq, the videos imported through that connection stay until you clear your library, disconnect YouTube in Sifteriq or delete your account, and the IDs of its channel and playlists stay until you disconnect YouTube in Sifteriq or delete your account. Today the same applies to a playlist you replace by choosing a different one for Sifteriq to sync, for example with Change playlist in Settings → Connections: the videos imported from it, and its ID, stay in the same way. At launch If a disconnect's deletion cannot finish at once, for example while a sync is running, it completes within 7 calendar days. If you revoke access on Google's security settings page instead, Sifteriq detects it and deletes that data within 30 calendar days; reconnecting the same account before then keeps it. Switching to a different Google account in Sifteriq ends the previous account's connection in the same way: unless you switch back to that account within 3 days, its data is deleted within 7 calendar days of the switch.

Deleting your data

You can delete the data Sifteriq stores at any time:

  • Clear library (Settings → Data and privacy) deletes your imported videos, notes, decisions, watch plans, saved exports, import history and the AI results for your videos. It keeps:
    • your account, YouTube connection, sync history, YouTube quota use and security events;
    • if you agreed to the AI notice of 13 October 2026, the goals you used recently, with their AI profiles, until they are deleted as described in How long we keep information;
    • usage information about past AI requests, such as token counts and cost, which is needed to enforce usage limits.
    It also deletes the rest of those AI request records and the counts of failed AI attempts within 7 calendar days.
  • Delete account data (Settings → Data and privacy) asks Google to revoke Sifteriq's access, deletes your library, exports, YouTube credentials and account, and signs you out on every device.
  • By email: write to abdoelsmany66@gmail.com from the address on your account.

Both in-app actions ask you to confirm, and to have signed in with your password within the last 10 minutes. They take effect immediately.

When you ask us to delete your data, or you delete your account, we delete the related data we store as soon as possible and within 7 calendar days. Two kinds of record are exceptions: the public video details that your imports stored, which are stored once for every user who saved the same video and follow the 30-day rule (see below); and our sign-in provider's own service logs, which keep entries for a short period set by our Supabase plan (see Sign-in records below). Records that show a deletion happened are also kept; they identify no one (see below).

Backups made by our database provider are kept for no more than 7 days, so deleted data also leaves them within that time.

Deleting the data Sifteriq stores does not affect any data stored by YouTube. To delete data on YouTube, use YouTube or another authorized app that can delete it.

Public video details, such as titles, descriptions, tags and channel names, are stored once and reused for every user who saved the same video; other users cannot see that you saved it. After you delete your account they are no longer linked to you. They follow the 30-day rule below.

Sifteriq keeps two of its own records after account deletion: a record that your library was cleared, which holds counts of what was deleted; and a record that the account was deleted, which holds the date, counts of what was deleted and whether Google confirmed the revocation. Neither holds your email address or any other identifier, any content, or any data from YouTube. They are kept with no time limit, to show the deletion happened.

If Google does not confirm the revocation, Sifteriq tells you and links to the Google security settings page so you can remove its access yourself.

Sign-in records. Our sign-in provider, Supabase, keeps a log of account events: sign-ups, sign-ins, session refreshes, sign-outs, password resets and changes, and the deletion of your account. Each entry holds your email address. Supabase keeps this log only in its own service logs, not in our database. Those logs also record IP addresses and browser types, and keep entries for a short period set by our Supabase plan, including the entry for the deletion of your account.

30-day refresh or deletion of data from YouTube

Sifteriq keeps data it obtains from YouTube API Services for no more than 30 calendar days before it refreshes that data from YouTube or deletes it. This covers video details, channel and playlist details, and copies of that data inside Sifteriq, such as watch-plan snapshots. Details of videos that become private, are deleted from YouTube, or are no longer in anyone's library are deleted from Sifteriq.

Today there are three exceptions:

  • Ended connections and replaced playlists. When you choose a different playlist for Sifteriq to sync, for example with Change playlist in Settings → Connections, or a YouTube connection ends other than through Disconnect YouTube, because Sifteriq's access ended at Google or you switched to a different Google account in Sifteriq, Sifteriq keeps the IDs of the playlists it no longer syncs and of an ended connection's channel, and the list of videos imported from those playlists, as described in How to revoke access. Their names and other details from YouTube still follow the rule above.
  • Saved export files. A file can no longer be downloaded 23 hours after it is made. The file itself is then deleted by a cleanup that runs in Sifteriq's background worker, which today runs only on the developer's computer, or when you make another export, clear your library, disconnect YouTube or delete your account. While none of these happens, a file can stay longer than 30 days (see Saved export files in How long we keep information).
  • The cached copy of your library. Sifteriq's pages read your library from a cached copy, which is rebuilt from the refreshed or deleted details when the background worker runs or you open Sifteriq. While neither happens, it can keep video details, such as titles and descriptions, for longer than 30 days.

At launch The data of an ended connection is deleted as described in How to revoke access, each saved export file is deleted within 24 hours after it is made, and the data of a playlist you replaced and the cached copy of your library follow the rule above.

The list of videos you imported from a file or pasted links is your own data, as are your notes, decisions and goal. The videos, notes and decisions stay until you clear your library or delete your account, and your goal until you delete your account. You can edit or clear a note, and edit your goal, at any time. When the details they are based on change, AI results are rebuilt if AI triage is on. A replaced result is deleted within 30 days after the cached copy of your library is rebuilt with its replacement.

AI processing

If you turn on AI triage, Sifteriq uses a large language model to suggest what to do with each video, for example watch now, watch this week, keep for reference, or skip. Videos are sent in batches of up to 20.

What is sent

  • For each video: title, channel name, duration, publish year and how old the video is (under 12 months, 12 to 18 months, or over 18 months), up to 16 of its YouTube tags, and the first 1,200 characters of the description. Each video is labeled with a temporary number (v01, v02 and so on), not its YouTube ID.
  • For each batch: the goal you wrote.
  • Never sent: your name, email address, user ID, YouTube channel, video IDs, playlist names, notes or uploaded files.

If you agree to the AI notice of 13 October 2026, Sifteriq also sends what Embeddings, video summaries and goal ranking describes.

Embeddings, video summaries and goal ranking

Sifteriq has more AI features that run only if you agreed to its AI notice of 13 October 2026 or a later one, which names each request below and who receives it. Sifteriq shows that notice when you turn on AI triage, or asks you to review it in Settings → Account if you turned AI triage on under an earlier notice; AI triage keeps working either way. Today Sifteriq offers that notice only to its developer's account, and every other account is offered the earlier notice, without these features. With your agreement, Sifteriq also sends:

  • For embeddings, to DeepInfra or Nebius: for each video, its title, its chapter titles, the first 500 characters of its description (without links, hashtags or timestamps) and up to 16 of its YouTube tags; and your goal on its own. They turn each text into a list of numbers (an embedding). Sifteriq uses these to compare videos with each other and with your goal, for example to find duplicate saves.
  • For video summaries: the details listed in What is sent for each video, without your goal, so the AI writes a short summary of it: its topic, names it mentions, what it covers, its type, its level and how time-sensitive it is.
  • For your goal: your goal on its own, so the AI turns it into a short profile of what the goal asks for, such as its topics, level and preferred length.
  • For ranking: your goal with Sifteriq's summaries of your videos and each video's length and age, so the AI ranks your videos for the goal. Titles and descriptions are not sent with these requests.
  • For duplicate checks: for two videos that may be the same, each one's title, length, publish year and up to 500 characters of its description, with whether they share a channel and a length, so the AI judges whether they are copies.

The requests for summaries, goal profiles, rankings and duplicate checks go through OpenRouter to the same AI model providers as AI triage; the embedding requests go through OpenRouter only to DeepInfra or Nebius (see Who processes it). None of these requests includes anything listed under "Never sent" above. Sifteriq stores the results with your library and deletes them as described in How long we keep information. It never shows or exports the embeddings. Where it shows the summaries, rankings or duplicate checks, they are its own estimates (see AI labels). Turning AI triage off stops all of these requests.

Who processes it

OpenRouter, Inc. (United States), an AI gateway, forwards each request to a model provider that runs the model and returns the result. OpenRouter also sorts a small sample of requests into anonymous categories, using a model that keeps no data, for its public usage rankings. Sifteriq does not train any AI model on your data.

OpenRouter sends each request for AI triage, video summaries, goal profiles, rankings and duplicate checks to one of four model providers: Together AI, Makora, Ionstream and Parasail. Together AI, Ionstream and Parasail are based in the United States; Makora is run by A2 Labs Inc., whose privacy policy names service providers in the United States. It sends each embedding request to one of two model providers: DeepInfra, run by DeepInfra, Inc., based in the United States; or Nebius, run by Nebius Group N.V., based in the Netherlands, whose privacy policy names group companies in the Netherlands, Israel and the United States. Sifteriq asks for zero data retention on every request, so OpenRouter sends it only to endpoints that OpenRouter lists as keeping no prompts or outputs after responding. Your data is therefore not stored by these model providers or used by them to train AI models.

You can also upload a decisions file produced by your own AI tool (Settings → Advanced). Sifteriq stores the suggestions in it as described in Information we collect. That file is not sent to OpenRouter or any model provider.

Your choice

AI triage is off until you turn it on. Sifteriq asks before it first sends your videos to an AI provider, and you can turn it off at any time with the AI recommendations switch in Settings → Account. Sifteriq then sends no new requests. Recommendations it already made stay in your library and are kept as described in How long we keep information. Without AI triage you can still import, organize and review your videos yourself.

When AI triage is on, your goal goes to the AI provider with each batch, and to the AI providers described in Embeddings, video summaries and goal ranking if you agreed to that notice. Please do not put sensitive personal information in it, such as health details, religious or political views, or anything that identifies you.

AI labels are Sifteriq's own estimates

Sifteriq's recommendations, priority scores, topics, reasons, video summaries, rankings and duplicate checks are generated independently by Sifteriq and are not sourced directly from YouTube's API. YouTube does not provide or endorse them. They can be wrong, so check them before you act on them, especially before removing videos from YouTube. Sifteriq labels these fields in the app and in exports as its own estimates, not data from YouTube; results from a decisions file you upload are labeled as imported from that file.

AI triage classifies videos, not people. The profile of a goal describes what the goal asks for, not you. Sifteriq does not use AI to profile you or to infer sensitive characteristics about you. Watch plans are built by fixed rules, without AI.

Your rights and choices

Depending on where you live, for example in the EU, the UK or California, data protection law gives you rights over your personal information. We offer the same choices to everyone:

  • Access and portability: export your library as CSV, JSON or Markdown in Sifteriq, or ask us for a copy of your information.
  • Correction: edit your display name, goal and notes in Sifteriq, or ask us to correct something.
  • Deletion: clear your library, delete your account, or email us (see Deleting your data).
  • Withdrawing consent: disconnect YouTube, revoke access in your Google account, or turn off AI recommendations (AI triage) in Settings → Account. This does not affect processing that already took place.
  • Objection and restriction: object to processing based on our legitimate interests, or ask us to limit how we use your information.
  • Complaints: you can complain to the data protection authority where you live or work.

To use any of these rights, email abdoelsmany66@gmail.com. We reply within one month, and may ask you to confirm that the account is yours. Using your rights will never affect how we treat you.

Security

We protect your information with measures that include:

  • Google refresh tokens encrypted at rest (AES-256-GCM) and never readable from the browser;
  • database row-level security, so each account can read only its own data;
  • administrative keys kept on the server only;
  • protection against cross-site request forgery and clickjacking, and a strict content security policy;
  • a recent password sign-in before you can clear your library or delete your account;
  • limits on upload sizes and request rates;
  • At launch HTTPS encryption for every connection to the app.

No system is perfectly secure. If a security breach affects your personal information, we will tell you, and the relevant authorities, where the law requires it.

When people can see your data

Sifteriq is run by one developer, who has administrative access to its database in order to operate the service. We do not read your information, including data from your Google account, except:

  • when you ask us to, for example to help with a support request;
  • when it is needed for security, such as investigating abuse or a bug;
  • to comply with the law;
  • in aggregated, anonymized form for running the service, such as total usage counts.
